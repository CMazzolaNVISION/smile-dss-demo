Participant: Lena Demo (Germany)
ⓘ About this view
What goes with feeling better or worse than usual? Three views.

SINCE THE LAST OBSERVATION: what happened between two observations (an event, negative thinking, each coping strategy) and how each emotion changed from the previous observation compared with the person's usual change: linear mixed models (this person's data, most participants, combined), trained with ESM Analytics.

EMOTION NETWORK: how each emotion at one observation goes with the emotions at the next observation (at most 24 h later), for happy, sad, anxious, stressed, relaxed, enthusiastic, insecure: a multilevel VAR, estimated the same way (this person's data, most participants, combined).

PREDICTOR LANDSCAPE: an Explainable Boosting Machine (EBM) that sorts each observation into worse than usual / similar / better than usual (overall mood vs the person's median of the previous 14 days, +-0.5 point, included in similar) from the last observation, the past 7 days, engagement, sleep and timing. It is a COHORT model: the same curves for everyone.

READ AS ASSOCIATIONS: the data are observational; the dashboard does not say that one thing causes the other, and it is not an early-warning tool.

What goes with Lena Demo feeling better or worse than usual: what happened since the last observation, and what was known before the observation. 152 mood observations over 42 days. These are associations, not causes.

How the numbers are made

ⓘ About this view
Emotion network: up to seven emotions (happy, sad, anxious, stressed, relaxed, enthusiastic, insecure; those answered at fewer than 50% of the training observations are left out). For each person, each emotion now is regressed on all of them at the previous complete observation (at most 24 h before, nights included), all centred on the person's own means (within-person). The cohort average of each arrow and how much participants differ (one random effect per arrow) come from the training participants; the person's estimate combines both, weighted by how much data they have.

EBM: interpret's ExplainableBoostingClassifier, no interactions, rare classes weighted up so that worse / better count as much as similar. Inputs: insecure, sad, anxious, irritated and relaxed at the last observation (at most 24 h before); median and variability (SD) of overall mood over the past 7 days; app engagement (mood observations in the past 7 days: none, 1-4, 5-10, 11+); game played in the past 7 days; sleep quality last night and over the last 5 nights (at least 3); weekend; time of day (local: morning < 12, afternoon 12-17, evening 17-20, night 20-24). Missing inputs have their own score.

1 · Since the last observation: events, thinking, coping

Change of the emotion from the previous observation vs this person's usual change

What happened between two observations

ⓘ About this view
The three "since the last observation" models: Events (important or stressful event; coping with the event, score), Thinking (repetitive negative thinking score; expecting something unpleasant), Coping (the six emotion-regulation strategies, each on its own: kept calm by breathing / relaxing muscles, thought about feelings over and over, talked to others, turned attention away, changed the way of thinking, tried not to show emotions).

Each number: how much MORE the emotion changed from the previous observation when it happened (or per point of a score), compared with the person's usual change, taking the previous emotion and the other predictors of the model into account.

Forest plot (default, overall mood): dot and 95% range of this person (full colour = clear or strong, faded = uncertain, dashed range = differs from most participants), grey diamond and thin range = most participants, vertical line = the usual change. "All emotions" shows the same as a heatmap for every emotion.

Models: Events · Thinking · Coping, on 151 pairs of observations at most 24 h apart. Each takes the emotion at the previous observation into account (people ruminate or use strategies because they already feel bad). Example: −0.8 for "an important or stressful event" = after an event, overall mood dropped 0.8 points more than usual between two observations. + = better, − = worse (negative feelings reversed).

How one number was made: Thought about feelings over and over → overall mood (Coping model)
  1. This person's data: From one observation to the next, overall mood changed by -0.2 on the 23 intervals with thought about feelings over and over and by +0.0 on the 89 without; taking the previous observation into account (and the other predictors of the Coping model): -0.2.
  2. Most participants (84 in training): Average effect -0.3.
  3. Combined: 7% from this person's data, 93% from the cohort: -0.3 (95% range -0.4 to -0.2), strong.
After thought about feelings over and over, this person ended up about 0.3 points lower overall mood than after a usual interval between two observations (worse).

Since the last observation: how much can you trust this?

  • 151 intervals between two observations
    Pairs of consecutive observations at most 24 hours apart, with overall mood at both. Fewer than 40 = too few, 40-79 = limited, 80 or more = good.
  • On average 12 % of each estimate comes from this person's data
    The rest comes from the cohort average. It is low where the person has few observations with a situation, or where participants react alike (then the cohort is a good guide).
  • 10 of 10 events, thinking and coping answers could be estimated
    Each needs at least 5 observations with it and 5 without (scores: 10 answers).

Tested on participants the models had never seen: estimated on their first 70% of days, compared with their remaining days. "Held" = pointed the same way later. 50% = a coin flip.

2 · Emotion network

An emotion at one observation → the emotions at the next observation

How emotions carry over

ⓘ About this view
Arrow A -> B: when this person feels 1 point better than usual on A at an observation, B tends to be this much better (+) or worse (-) at the next observation, taking the other six emotions into account. As everywhere in the DSS, negative feelings are reversed (+ = better): "anxious (reversed) -> sad (reversed) +0.2" = less anxious goes with less sad later. Only strong or clear findings are drawn (at most 12); width = size; colour = + (blue) / - (orange); dashed = differs from most participants. Ring around a node = carry-over (A -> A), how much an emotion stays. "Most participants (cohort)" shows the cohort's arrows. "Table" shows every arrow with its number (rows: previous observation, columns: now).

Arrow A → B = when this person feels better than usual on A at an observation, B tends to be better (+) or worse (−) at the next observation (points per point). Negative feelings are reversed, as everywhere in the DSS: + = better, − = worse. Only clear findings are drawn. Ring = carry-over: how much an emotion stays from one observation to the next.

How one number was made: Stressed (reversed) (previous observation) → insecure (reversed) (now)
  1. This person's data: 151 pairs of observations at most 24 h apart; insecure (reversed) now regressed on the 7 emotions at the previous observation, all centred on the person's usual levels: +0.21 per point.
  2. Most participants (84 in training): Average +0.05.
  3. Combined: 24% from this person's data, 76% from most participants: +0.09 (95% range +0.02 to +0.15), clear; differs from most participants. The person's own estimate weighs little: participants are alike here, or there are few pairs.
When this person is 1 point less stressed than usual at an observation (better), at the next observation they tend to be about 0.1 points less insecure than usual (better).

ⓘ About this view

Which inputs matter: cohort vs this participant

ⓘ About this view
Importance = how much an input moves the EBM's prediction on average (mean absolute contribution, the EBM's own definition, averaged over the three classes). Diamond = across the training participants; dot = across this person's observations. The EBM is the same for everyone: a different dot only means this person's VALUES of that input differ (e.g. their sleep varies more), not that they react differently.

◆ = how much each input moves the EBM's prediction across the training participants; ● = across this person's observations. A big gap means this person's values are unusual for that input (e.g. their sleep varies much more or much less): the model itself is the same.

How the most important inputs push the prediction

ⓘ About this view
The EBM's curve of the most important inputs (cohort or this person): for each value, how much it pushes the prediction towards better than usual (above 0) or worse than usual (below 0), in the model's units (log-odds of better vs worse). Inputs answered in whole points (last-observation emotions, shown on -3 to +3 with negative feelings reversed; sleep last night, 1-7) and categories have one bar per answer, with the number of this person's observations on top; averages (past week, last 5 nights) have a curve with this person's values as ticks. Error bars / grey band = uncertainty.

The EBM's curves (the same for everyone): above 0 = pushes towards better than usual, below 0 = towards worse; grey band = uncertainty. Ticks / observation counts = this person's own values.

How much can you trust this?

Checks for this participant

ⓘ About this view
Checks with thresholds: mood observations and network pairs (fewer than 40 too few, 40-79 limited, 80 or more good), observations with a usual level for the EBM (fewer than 20: no accuracy for this person), sleep (not enough below 50% of the observations), the cohort nature of the EBM, and whether the person was used for training. The table: network arrows estimated on the first 70% of days of participants never seen in training, compared with their remaining days; "held later" = same direction (50% = coin flip).
  • 152 mood observations over 42 days
    Fewer than 40 = too few, 40-79 = limited, 80 or more = good.
  • 151 pairs of observations for the emotion network
    Consecutive observations at most 24 h apart with all the network's emotions at the earlier one (111 of the 152 observations have all 7). Fewer than 40 = too few, 40-79 = limited, 80 or more = good.
  • 149 observations with a usual level for the EBM
    An observation needs at least 3 earlier observations in the previous 14 days. Fewer than 20: no accuracy for this person; 20-39: limited; 40 or more: good.
  • Sleep known for 95 % of the observations (40 nights)
    From the morning observation. On days without it, the sleep inputs are “missing” (the EBM has a score for that).
  • The EBM is a cohort model
    Its curves are the same for everyone. “This participant” importance shows which inputs move the prediction for them, given their own values, not that they react differently. How this person differs is in the emotion network.
  • This participant was kept out of training
    The accuracy on their observations is a fair test, as for a new patient.
