ⓘ About this view
How do the participant's emotions relate to where they are, what they do and who they are with at the moment of the beep? And how much can that be trusted? (What happened since the previous beep -- events, negative thinking, coping -- is in Mood Change Analytics.)

Every number comes from a statistical model -- a linear mixed model, one per type of question and per emotion (Where, Doing, With) -- in three steps: (1) the participant's own beeps, compared with themselves; (2) the average of the training participants (same model, trained offline); (3) the two combined, weighted by how much data the participant has, with a 95% range.

Numbers are points on the 1-7 scale and follow the DSS rule: + = positive valence (better), − = negative valence (worse), whatever the emotion; negative feelings are reversed (anxious (reversed) +0.6 = 0.6 points LESS anxious; their levels are shown as 8 − answer). Colors: blue = better, red = worse; faded / hollow = uncertain; bold = strong; dashed = differs from most participants.

READ AS ASSOCIATIONS: the data are observational; the dashboard does not say that one thing causes the other.

How the numbers are made

ⓘ About this view
The model is a linear mixed model (repeated measures), estimated in two steps.

Step 1, this person only. For each group (e.g. "at home"), the person's average emotion at the beeps in that group minus their average over all beeps (their usual level). (In Mood Change Analytics, SINCE THE LAST BEEP uses the same model with a regression of the emotion at the beep on the same emotion at the previous beep (at most 24 h before, nights included) and the predictors of the model together, so each effect is "change compared with the usual change", adjusted for the other predictors of the model.)

Step 2, the cohort. For every predictor and emotion, the average effect of the training participants and how much they differ (one random effect per predictor, no correlations). The person's estimate = weight x own effect + (1 - weight) x cohort average; the weight grows with the person's number of beeps and with how much participants differ.

Scores: repetitive negative thinking = mean of 3 items; coping with an event = mean of 5 items with the two "hard to understand / identify others' thoughts" items reversed (7 = good), only when an event happened.

ⓘ About this view
Rows: the groups of the three context models -- Where (at home, at work / school, at friends' / family's, elsewhere), Doing (working / studying, resting, active leisure, chores and self-care, social interaction), With (alone, close people, other people, online only). Columns: emotions.

Each number: how much higher (+) or lower (-) the emotion is at the beeps in that group than the person's usual level, in points (1-7). Blue = better, red = worse. Solid = clear or strong finding (bold = strong); faded = uncertain; dashed border = differs from most participants; blank = fewer than 5 beeps in the group.

Buttons: "Estimate (own + cohort)" (the number to use), "Own data only", "Cohort average". Hover a cell for the raw averages, the cohort average, the weight of the person's own data and the 95% range.

Example: +1.0 under Relaxed for "at home" = 1 point more relaxed at home than their usual level. Blank = too few beeps. Hover a cell for its numbers.
