DSS Prototype

The question
How is this participant's mood in daily life?
What it uses
The short mood questionnaires of the companion app, answered several times a day, and the questionnaires filled in at study visits.
How it answers
First how reliable the data are, then mood and sleep, then how mood changes over time and next to the questionnaires.
How to read it
Averages, and a smoothed line for the trend, compared with similar participants. Mood goes from −3 (negative) to +3 (positive).

ⓘ About this view
The selected mood over time, on the -3 (worse) to +3 (better) scale, in the study site's local time.

- Small squares: every observation, at its time.

- Dots: the daily average, at midday of the day (hover: SD and number of observations).

- Line through the observations: the smoothed trend (on by default), with its peaks (blue triangles up) and valleys (orange triangles down); the method is below.

- Grey band and thick line per week: the weekly average and SD, from Monday 00:00 to Sunday 23:59 (hover for the numbers).

- Grey dotted line and band (hidden at first: click its legend item): the cohort average and +/- 1 SD of the reference group chosen above the chart, i.e. the average of each participant's own average, and the SD between participants.

- "zzz" (hidden at first: click its legend item): sleep quality, from the morning answer to "I slept well last night" (-3 to +3, coloured like the mood), placed at midnight before the day it was answered, i.e. on the night it describes.

- Background: weekdays from midnight to midnight, lighter on Monday to darker on Sunday; dotted vertical lines at Monday 00:00. Diamonds under the scale: days with diary entries. The date ticks are at midday.

Click a legend item to hide or show it; use the slider under the chart to zoom on a period.


SMOOTHED TREND: LOWESS (locally weighted scatterplot smoothing) on every observation, not on the daily averages. For each observation, a straight line is fitted to the nearest observations in time, weighted by the tricube function w = (1 − (d / d_max)³)³ (d = distance in time, d_max = distance of the farthest observation used), and the trend is that line's value at the observation. The share of observations used for each fit is about 4 days of data: frac = 4 / days with observations (at least 5 observations, at most all). 1 robustness iteration: observations far from the first fit get a smaller weight (bisquare weights), so a single unusual answer does not bend the curve.

PEAKS AND VALLEYS: the local maxima (peaks, blue ▲) and minima (valleys, orange ▼) of the smoothed trend whose prominence is at least 0.5 points on the −3 to +3 scale. Prominence of a peak = how far it rises above the higher of the two lowest points between it and a higher part of the trend on each side (the topographic definition; scipy.signal.find_peaks); a valley is a peak of the trend turned upside down. Small wiggles below the threshold are ignored.
Shown in the last row: the mood on the day of each session (average and SD of that day's observations, −3 to +3), to read each questionnaire against the mood in the same column.

PROMs: questionnaire sessions

Patient-reported outcome measures. One row per questionnaire, one column per session, oldest on the left. The number under each score is the change since the previous session.

ⓘ About this view
One row per questionnaire (its name, what it measures, and whether a higher score is better or worse), one column per session, oldest on the left. A session groups the questionnaires filled in one after the other: a new session starts after a pause of more than 2 hours, or when a questionnaire is filled in a second time.

Each cell: the score on the questionnaire's own range, its severity band (where the questionnaire has bands) and, under it, the change since the previous session (coloured as an improvement or a worsening for that questionnaire). Skipped = completed in other sessions but not in this one; Incomplete = some items not answered.

Last row: the mood selected above on the day of the session, average and SD of that day's observations, on -3 to +3, so each questionnaire can be read against the mood in the same column.
