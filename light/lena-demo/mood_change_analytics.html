ⓘ About this view
What goes with feeling better or worse than usual? Three views.

SINCE THE LAST BEEP: what happened between two beeps (an event, negative thinking, each coping strategy) and how each emotion changed from the previous beep compared with the person's usual change -- linear mixed models (this person's data, most participants, combined), trained with ESM Analytics.

EMOTION NETWORK: how each emotion at one beep goes with the emotions at the next beep (at most 24 h later), for happy, sad, anxious, stressed, relaxed, enthusiastic, insecure -- a multilevel VAR, estimated the same way (this person's data, most participants, combined).

PREDICTOR LANDSCAPE: an Explainable Boosting Machine (EBM) that sorts each beep into worse than usual / similar / better than usual (mood valence vs the person's median of the previous 14 days, +-0.5 point, included in similar) from the last beep, the past 7 days, engagement, sleep and timing. It is a COHORT model: the same curves for everyone.

READ AS ASSOCIATIONS: the data are observational; the dashboard does not say that one thing causes the other, and it is not an early-warning tool.

What goes with Lena Demo feeling better or worse than usual: what happened since the last beep, and what was known before the beep. 152 mood beeps over 42 days. These are associations, not causes.

How the numbers are made

ⓘ About this view
Emotion network: up to seven emotions (happy, sad, anxious, stressed, relaxed, enthusiastic, insecure; those answered at fewer than 50% of the training beeps are left out). For each person, each emotion now is regressed on all of them at the previous complete beep (at most 24 h before, nights included), all centred on the person's own means (within-person). The cohort average of each arrow and how much participants differ (one random effect per arrow) come from the training participants; the person's estimate combines both, weighted by how much data they have.

EBM: interpret's ExplainableBoostingClassifier, no interactions, rare classes weighted up so that worse / better count as much as similar. Inputs: insecure, sad, anxious, irritated and relaxed at the last beep (at most 24 h before); median and variability (SD) of mood valence over the past 7 days; app engagement (mood beeps in the past 7 days: none, 1-4, 5-10, 11+); game played in the past 7 days; sleep quality last night and over the last 5 nights (at least 3); weekend; time of day (local: morning < 12, afternoon 12-17, evening 17-20, night 20-24). Missing inputs have their own score.

1 · Since the last beep: events, thinking, coping

Change of the emotion from the previous beep vs this person's usual change

What happened between two beeps

ⓘ About this view
The three "since the last beep" models: Events (important or stressful event; coping with the event, score), Thinking (repetitive negative thinking score; expecting something unpleasant), Coping (the six emotion-regulation strategies, each on its own: kept calm by breathing / relaxing muscles, thought about feelings over and over, talked to others, turned attention away, changed the way of thinking, tried not to show emotions).

Each number: how much MORE the emotion changed from the previous beep when it happened (or per point of a score), compared with the person's usual change, taking the previous emotion and the other predictors of the model into account.

Forest plot (default, mood valence): dot and 95% range of this person (filled = clear or strong, hollow grey = uncertain, dashed range = differs from most participants), diamond and thin range = most participants, vertical line = the usual change. "All emotions" shows the same as a heatmap for every emotion.

Models: Events · Thinking · Coping, on 151 pairs of beeps at most 24 h apart. Each takes the emotion at the previous beep into account (people ruminate or use strategies because they already feel bad). Example: −0.8 for "an important or stressful event" = after an event, mood valence (happy & sad) dropped 0.8 points more than usual between two beeps. + = better, − = worse (negative feelings reversed).

How one number was made — Thought about feelings over and over → mood valence (happy & sad) (Coping model)
  1. This person's data: From one beep to the next, mood valence (happy & sad) changed by -0.2 on the 23 intervals with thought about feelings over and over and by +0.0 on the 89 without; taking the previous beep into account (and the other predictors of the Coping model): -0.2.
  2. Most participants (84 in training): Average effect -0.3.
  3. Combined: 7% from this person's data, 93% from the cohort: -0.3 (95% range -0.4 to -0.2), strong.
After thought about feelings over and over, this person ended up about 0.3 points lower mood valence than after a usual interval between two beeps (worse).

Since the last beep: how much can you trust this?

  • 151 intervals between two beeps
    Pairs of consecutive beeps at most 24 hours apart, with mood valence at both. Fewer than 40 = too few, 40-79 = limited, 80 or more = good.
  • On average 12 % of each estimate comes from this person's data
    The rest comes from the cohort average. It is low where the person has few beeps with a situation, or where participants react alike (then the cohort is a good guide).
  • 10 of 10 events, thinking and coping answers could be estimated
    Each needs at least 5 beeps with it and 5 without (scores: 10 answers).

Tested on participants the models had never seen: estimated on their first 70% of days, compared with their remaining days. "Held" = pointed the same way later. 50% = a coin flip.

2 · Emotion network

An emotion at one beep → the emotions at the next beep

How emotions carry over

ⓘ About this view
Arrow A -> B: when this person feels 1 point better than usual on A at a beep, B tends to be this much better (+) or worse (-) at the next beep, taking the other six emotions into account. As everywhere in the DSS, negative feelings are reversed (+ = positive valence): "anxious (reversed) -> sad (reversed) +0.2" = less anxious goes with less sad later. Only strong or clear findings are drawn (at most 12); width = size; color = + (blue) / - (red); dashed = differs from most participants. Ring around a node = carry-over (A -> A), how much an emotion stays. "Most participants (cohort)" shows the cohort's arrows. "Table" shows every arrow with its number (rows: previous beep, columns: now).

Arrow A → B = when this person feels better than usual on A at a beep, B tends to be better (+) or worse (−) at the next beep (points per point). Negative feelings are reversed, as everywhere in the DSS: + = positive valence, − = negative valence. Only clear findings are drawn. Ring = carry-over: how much an emotion stays from one beep to the next.

How one number was made — Stressed (reversed) (previous beep) → insecure (reversed) (now)
  1. This person's data: 151 pairs of beeps at most 24 h apart; insecure (reversed) now regressed on the 7 emotions at the previous beep, all centred on the person's usual levels: +0.21 per point.
  2. Most participants (84 in training): Average +0.05.
  3. Combined: 24% from this person's data, 76% from most participants: +0.09 (95% range +0.02 to +0.15), clear; differs from most participants. The person's own estimate weighs little: participants are alike here, or there are few pairs.
When this person is 1 point less stressed than usual at a beep (better), at the next beep they tend to be about 0.1 points less insecure than usual (better).

Which inputs matter: cohort vs this participant

ⓘ About this view
Importance = how much an input moves the EBM's prediction on average (mean absolute contribution, the EBM's own definition, averaged over the three classes). Diamond = across the training participants; dot = across this person's beeps. The EBM is the same for everyone: a different dot only means this person's VALUES of that input differ (e.g. their sleep varies more), not that they react differently.

◆ = how much each input moves the EBM's prediction across the training participants; ● = across this person's beeps. A big gap means this person's values are unusual for that input (e.g. their sleep varies much more or much less) — the model itself is the same.

How the most important inputs push the prediction

ⓘ About this view
The EBM's curve of the most important inputs (cohort or this person): for each value, how much it pushes the prediction towards better than usual (above 0) or worse than usual (below 0), in the model's units (log-odds of better vs worse). Inputs answered in whole points (last-beep emotions, sleep last night) and categories have one bar per answer, with the number of this person's beeps on top; averages (past week, last 5 nights) have a curve with this person's values as ticks. Error bars / grey band = uncertainty.

The EBM's curves (the same for everyone): above 0 = pushes towards better than usual, below 0 = towards worse; grey band = uncertainty. Ticks / beep counts = this person's own values.

Checks for this participant

ⓘ About this view
Checks with thresholds: mood beeps and network pairs (fewer than 40 too few, 40-79 limited, 80 or more good), beeps with a usual level for the EBM (fewer than 20: no accuracy for this person), sleep (not enough below 50% of the beeps), the cohort nature of the EBM, and whether the person was used for training. The table: network arrows estimated on the first 70% of days of participants never seen in training, compared with their remaining days; "held later" = same direction (50% = coin flip).
  • 152 mood beeps over 42 days
    Fewer than 40 = too few, 40-79 = limited, 80 or more = good.
  • 151 pairs of beeps for the emotion network
    Consecutive beeps at most 24 h apart with all the network's emotions at the earlier one (111 of the 152 beeps have all 7). Fewer than 40 = too few, 40-79 = limited, 80 or more = good.
  • 149 beeps with a usual level for the EBM
    A beep needs at least 3 earlier beeps in the previous 14 days. Fewer than 20: no accuracy for this person; 20-39: limited; 40 or more: good.
  • Sleep known for 95 % of the beeps (40 nights)
    From the morning beep. On days without it, the sleep inputs are “missing” (the EBM has a score for that).
  • The EBM is a cohort model
    Its curves are the same for everyone. “This participant” importance shows which inputs move the prediction for them, given their own values -- not that they react differently. How this person differs is in the emotion network.
  • This participant was kept out of training
    The accuracy on their beeps is a fair test, as for a new patient.
