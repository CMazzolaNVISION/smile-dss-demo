ⓘ About this view
How do the participant's emotions relate to where they are, what they do and who they are with at the moment of the observation? And how much can that be trusted? (What happened since the previous observation, events, negative thinking, coping, is in Mood Change Analytics.)

Every number comes from a statistical model (a linear mixed model, one per type of question and per emotion: Where, Doing, With) in three steps: (1) the participant's own observations, compared with themselves; (2) the average of the training participants (same model, trained offline); (3) the two combined, weighted by how much data the participant has, with a 95% range.

Numbers are points on the -3 to +3 scale and follow the DSS rule: + = better, − = worse, whatever the emotion; negative feelings are reversed (anxious (reversed) +0.6 = 0.6 points LESS anxious; their levels are shown as 4 − answer, positive ones as answer − 4). Colours: blue = better, orange = worse; faded = uncertain; bold = strong; dashed = differs from most participants; grey diamond = most participants.

READ AS ASSOCIATIONS: the data are observational; the dashboard does not say that one thing causes the other.

How the numbers are made

ⓘ About this view
The model is a linear mixed model (repeated measures), estimated in two steps.

Step 1, this person only. For each group (e.g. "at home"), the person's average emotion at the observations in that group minus their average over all observations (their usual level). (In Mood Change Analytics, SINCE THE LAST OBSERVATION uses the same model with a regression of the emotion at the observation on the same emotion at the previous observation (at most 24 h before, nights included) and the predictors of the model together, so each effect is "change compared with the usual change", adjusted for the other predictors of the model.)

Step 2, the cohort. For every predictor and emotion, the average effect of the training participants and how much they differ (one random effect per predictor, no correlations). The person's estimate = weight x own effect + (1 - weight) x cohort average; the weight grows with the person's number of observations and with how much participants differ.

Scores: repetitive negative thinking = mean of 3 items; coping with an event = mean of 5 items with the two "hard to understand / identify others' thoughts" items reversed (7 = good), only when an event happened.

ⓘ About this view
Rows: the groups of the three context models: Where (at home, at work / school, at friends' / family's, elsewhere), Doing (working / studying, resting, active leisure, chores and self-care, social interaction), With (alone, close people, other people, online only). Columns: emotions.

Each number: how much higher (+) or lower (-) the emotion is at the observations in that group than the person's usual level, in points (-3 to +3 scale). Blue = better, orange = worse. Solid = clear or strong finding (bold = strong); faded = uncertain; dashed border = differs from most participants; blank = fewer than 5 observations in the group.

Buttons: "Estimate (own + cohort)" (the number to use), "Own data only", "Cohort average". Hover a cell for the raw averages, the cohort average, the weight of the person's own data and the 95% range.

Example: +1.0 under Relaxed for "at home" = 1 point more relaxed at home than their usual level. Blank = too few observations. Hover a cell for its numbers.

Checks for this participant

ⓘ About this view
Plain checks of the data behind these numbers, with thresholds: mood observations (fewer than 40 too few, 40-79 limited, 80 or more good), share of each estimate from the person's own data, situations that could be estimated, and whether the person was used to train the cohort average.

The table: the models were tested on participants they had never seen (cross-validation by participant). Effects were estimated on their first 70% of days and compared with their remaining days. "Held later" = share of findings pointing the same way later (50% = coin flip), per type of finding. The last column repeats the test on this person (a fair test only if they were kept out of training).
  • 135 mood observations over 42 days
    Fewer than 40 = too few, 40-79 = limited, 80 or more = good. In testing, for participants with 80 or more observations, 74 % of the solid-color findings held in their later days.
  • On average 8 % of each estimate comes from this person's data
    The rest comes from the cohort average. It is low where the person has few observations with a situation, or where participants react alike (then the cohort is a good guide).
  • 11 of 13 situations could be estimated
    Each needs at least 5 observations with it and 5 without (scores: 10 answers). Too rare for this person (cohort average only): at friends' / family's, online only.
